# Authorization Model

The Stellar Subscriptions authorization model is designed around a core principle: **subscribers grant bounded, revocable permission** to merchants to charge their account over time. This page explains exactly what you authorize when you subscribe, and how the system prevents abuse.

## The Problem with Traditional Subscriptions

Traditional subscription systems (web2) typically work one of two ways:

1. **Store credit card details** — the merchant can charge you at will
2. **Unbounded blockchain allowances** — you approve unlimited token transfers

Both models share a dangerous flaw: **you lose control after the initial authorization**. The merchant can charge any amount, at any frequency, and you have no on-chain protection.

## Our Solution: Bounded Authorization

When you subscribe on Stellar, you grant an authorization with **three hard limits** enforced by smart contracts:

### The Three Invariants

<CalloutBox type="info">
**Invariant 1: Total Cap**
The merchant can never charge you more than the cap amount you specified, across the entire lifetime of your subscription.
</CalloutBox>

<CalloutBox type="info">
**Invariant 2: Billing Interval**
The merchant can only charge you once per billing interval (measured in Stellar ledgers). Attempts to charge more frequently will fail.
</CalloutBox>

<CalloutBox type="info">
**Invariant 3: Revocation**
You can cancel your subscription at any time, immediately revoking the merchant's ability to charge you. Once cancelled, all future charge attempts fail.
</CalloutBox>

These invariants are **enforced on-chain** by the subscription contract's `charge()` function. Even a malicious merchant cannot bypass them.

## Worked Example: 120 USDC Cap, 10 USDC/month

Let's walk through a concrete subscription to understand exactly what happens.

### Initial Setup

You want to subscribe to a service that costs **10 USDC per month**. You decide to authorize **12 months** worth of charges, so you set:

- **Amount per charge**: 10 USDC
- **Billing interval**: 2,592,000 ledgers (~30 days at 1 ledger/second)
- **Cap**: 120 USDC (10 × 12 months)

When you call `subscribe()`, the contract stores these values and grants the merchant permission to call `charge()` under these constraints.

### Month 1: First Charge

The merchant calls `charge()` on ledger 1,000,000. The contract checks:

1. ✅ Has at least one interval passed since subscription start? **Yes** (this is the first charge)
2. ✅ Is `total_charged + 10 USDC ≤ 120 USDC`? **Yes** (0 + 10 = 10 ≤ 120)
3. ✅ Is the subscription active (not cancelled/paused)? **Yes**

All checks pass. The contract:
- Transfers 10 USDC from your account to the merchant
- Updates `total_charged = 10 USDC`
- Sets `last_charge_ledger = 1,000,000`
- Calculates `next_charge_ledger = 1,000,000 + 2,592,000 = 3,592,000`

### Month 2-11: Normal Charges

Each month, the merchant charges 10 USDC. After 11 charges, `total_charged = 110 USDC`.

### Month 12: Final Charge (Cap Nearly Reached)

On ledger ~31,000,000, the merchant calls `charge()` again. The contract checks:

1. ✅ Interval check passes
2. ✅ `110 + 10 = 120 ≤ 120` (exactly at cap)
3. ✅ Subscription active

The charge succeeds. Now `total_charged = 120 USDC`.

### Month 13: Cap Exhausted

The merchant tries to charge again on ledger ~33,500,000. The contract checks:

1. ✅ Interval check passes
2. ❌ `120 + 10 = 130 > 120` **FAILS**

The charge is **rejected**. The subscription status is automatically set to `Exhausted`. No tokens are transferred. **The merchant cannot extract more than the cap, ever.**

### What About Earlier Cancellation?

If you had called `cancel()` at any point (say, after month 5), the contract would:
- Set subscription status to `Cancelled`
- From that moment, all `charge()` calls fail immediately
- Your remaining cap (70 USDC in this case) is protected

## Protection Against Malicious Merchants

### Scenario 1: Merchant Tries to Charge Too Often

The merchant calls `charge()` twice in the same day (before the interval elapses). The second call:
- Fails the interval check: `current_ledger < next_charge_ledger`
- Reverts with error: `IntervalNotElapsed`
- No tokens transferred

### Scenario 2: Merchant Calls a Modified Contract

The merchant deploys their own version of the charge function that skips cap checks. But:
- They don't control *your* subscription contract instance
- The authorization you granted is specific to the legitimate contract
- Their modified contract has no permission to transfer your tokens
- Attack fails at the Stellar protocol level

### Scenario 3: Merchant Tries to Front-Run Your Cancellation

You submit a `cancel()` transaction. The merchant sees it in the mempool and tries to submit a `charge()` first. Even if their transaction is processed first:
- Your `cancel()` still goes through immediately after
- They get at most one more charge (which was within their authorized interval)
- The cap still applies to that charge
- Future charges fail as expected

This is not ideal UX, but it's **bounded damage** — they can't drain your cap before you revoke.

## Pause vs Cancel

Pausing a subscription is different from cancelling:

### Pause
- Sets status to `Paused`
- Prevents charges while paused
- Can be resumed by calling `resume()`
- When resumed, the next charge ledger is recalculated from the resume point
- Useful for temporary holds (e.g., going on vacation)

### Cancel
- Sets status to `Cancelled`
- Prevents all future charges permanently
- **Cannot be undone** — cancellation is final
- The subscription record remains on-chain but is inactive
- Useful when you no longer want the service

<CalloutBox type="warning">
**Important:** Pausing does *not* extend your cap. If you paused for 3 months and then resumed, you still have the same total cap. The billing interval simply shifts forward.
</CalloutBox>

## Why This Is Safer

Traditional blockchain token approvals (like ERC-20 `approve()`) grant **unlimited access** until explicitly revoked. The merchant can transfer any amount at any time.

Our model inverts this:
- **Default state is no access** — the merchant must call `charge()` each time
- **Each charge is validated** against cap, interval, and status
- **Revocation is instant** — no waiting period, no merchant cooperation required
- **Caps are absolute** — even buggy or malicious contract logic can't exceed them

## Summary

When you subscribe, you're not giving the merchant your tokens. You're granting them **permission to request** tokens, subject to:

1. A total lifetime cap
2. A minimum time between charges
3. Your ability to instantly revoke

These guarantees are enforced by the Stellar network itself, not by the merchant's honor system.
